# UK Literature

## 1. King Arthur and the Knights of the Round Table

### Historical Background and Origin
* Rooted in ancient British legends about a 5th/6th-century Celtic leader resisting Anglo-Saxon invaders.
* Popularized in medieval chivalric literature by Sir Thomas Malory in *Le Morte d'Arthur* (1485).

### Genre and Setting
* **Genre:** Chivalric romance / heroic medieval legend.
* **Setting:** Mythical medieval Britain, centered around King Arthur's court at **Camelot**.

### Main Characters
* **King Arthur:** The virtuous, honorable king of Britain.
* **Merlin:** Wise wizard, prophet, and Arthur's magical mentor.
* **Queen Guinevere:** Arthur's queen.
* **Sir Lancelot:** Arthur's bravest and greatest knight, who falls in love with Guinevere.
* **Mordred:** Traitorous nephew/son of Arthur who brings down Camelot.
* **The Lady of the Lake:** Water fairy who gives Arthur his magical sword, **Excalibur**.

### Plot Summary
* Young boy Arthur pulls a magical sword from an anvil, proving his rightful royal bloodline.
* Arthur builds the court of **Camelot** and creates the **Round Table** (where all knights sit as equals).
* Knights set off on holy adventures, notably the quest to find the **Holy Grail**.
* The kingdom collapses when Lancelot and Guinevere's secret romance is revealed and Mordred rebels.
* Arthur and Mordred slay each other at the **Battle of Camlann**.
* Excalibur is returned to the lake, and the dying Arthur is carried to the enchanted isle of **Avalon** ("The Once and Future King").

### Main Ideas and Moral Message
* Chivalric ideals (honor, courage, brotherhood, and protection of the weak).
* Human weakness, betrayal, and the tragedy of an ideal kingdom falling from within.

## 2. Robin Hood

### Historical Background and Origin
* English folklore originating from medieval folk ballads of the 13th and 14th centuries.
* Passed down by oral tradition before being recorded in print.

### Genre and Setting
* **Genre:** Folk legend / adventure outlaw story.
* **Setting:** 12th-century medieval England, specifically **Sherwood Forest** and the city of **Nottingham**, during the reign of King Richard the Lionheart.

### Main Characters
* **Robin Hood:** Highly skilled archer and outlaw leader who *"robs from the rich and gives to the poor"*.
* **Maid Marian:** Noblewoman and Robin's true love.
* **Little John:** Robin's loyal, giant second-in-command (ironic nickname).
* **Friar Tuck:** Jovial, food-loving friar.
* **Sheriff of Nottingham:** The greedy, corrupt local ruler who oppresses and overtaxes the poor.

### Plot Summary
* Robin is unjustly stripped of his lands and declared an outlaw.
* Flees into the deep forest of Sherwood, gathering a band of outlaws known as the **Merry Men**.
* They ambush corrupt officials and wealthy churchmen travelling through the woods, redistributing the stolen wealth to starving peasants.
* The Sheriff hosts an archery contest with a golden arrow as a trap to capture Robin.
* Robin enters disguised, wins the tournament, and escapes.
* King Richard I returns from the Crusades in disguise, pardons Robin Hood, and restores his title.

### Main Ideas and Moral Message
* Social justice, loyalty, and resistance against political corruption and tyranny.

## 3. Geoffrey Chaucer – The Canterbury Tales (The Wife of Bath)

### Author and Background
* Written by **Geoffrey Chaucer** (c. 1340–1400), known as the **"Father of English Literature"**.
* First great English author to write in Middle English vernacular rather than Latin or Norman French.
* *(Note: Some school lists mix up this work with 20th-century playwright George Bernard Shaw; Shaw wrote Pygmalion, while Canterbury Tales was written by Chaucer).*

### Genre and Frame Story
* **Genre:** Verse frame narrative / collection of pilgrim tales.
* **Setting:** Late 14th century; 29 pilgrims journey together from the Tabard Inn in Southwark (London) to the shrine of Saint Thomas Becket at **Canterbury Cathedral**.
* Each pilgrim tells stories to pass the time on the road.

### The Wife of Bath (*Alison*)
* A wealthy, experienced cloth-maker from the town of Bath.
* Married five times; defends female independence, self-worth, and sexual autonomy with wit and boldness.

### Plot of The Wife of Bath's Tale
* Set in the mythical days of King Arthur.
* A young knight commits a crime (rapes a maiden) and is sentenced to death.
* Queen Guinevere gives him one year and a day to answer the question: **"What do women desire most?"**
* He travels the realm asking women, but receives conflicting answers (money, beauty, clothes, flattery).
* On his final day, he encounters a hideous old hag in the forest who promises him the correct answer if he grants her first request.
* In front of the court, he reveals the secret: **Women desire sovereignty (*sovereynetee*) and mastery over their husbands and lives.** The court agrees, and his life is spared.
* The hag demands that the knight marry her.
* On their wedding night, she asks him to choose: she can be ugly and faithful, or young, beautiful, and unfaithful.
* The knight yields the choice to her: *"You choose, my lady."*
* Because he granted her sovereignty, the curse is broken, and she transforms into a beautiful, loyal wife.

### Main Ideas and Moral Message
* Equality and female autonomy in marriage.
* Inner virtue and true nobility (*gentilesse*) matter far more than outward looks or social rank.

## 4. William Shakespeare – Romeo and Juliet

### Author and Historical Context
* Written by **William Shakespeare** (1564–1616) around 1595 during the Elizabethan Renaissance.
* Staged at the open-air Globe Theatre in London.

### Genre and Setting
* **Genre:** Romantic tragedy in blank verse and rhyming couplets.
* **Setting:** 14th-century **Verona** and Mantua, Italy.

### Main Characters
* **Romeo Montague:** Passionate, impulsive young nobleman.
* **Juliet Capulet:** 13-year-old daughter of the rival family; intelligent, courageous, and loyal.
* **Friar Laurence:** Franciscan monk who secretly marries the lovers in hopes of ending the feud.
* **Tybalt:** Hot-tempered cousin of Juliet ("Prince of Cats").
* **Mercutio:** Witty, talkative best friend of Romeo.
* **The Nurse:** Juliet's devoted, humorous caretaker.

### Plot Summary
* An ancient family feud rages between the **Montagues** and the **Capulets**.
* Romeo crashes a Capulet masked ball and falls instantly in love with Juliet; they declare their love during the famous **balcony scene** and are secretly married by Friar Laurence.
* In a street duel, Tybalt kills Mercutio; an enraged Romeo slays Tybalt and is banished to Mantua.
* Juliet's parents order her to marry Count Paris.
* Desperate, Juliet takes a sleeping potion from Friar Laurence that makes her appear dead for 42 hours.
* The messenger fails to reach Romeo; believing Juliet has truly died, Romeo buys deadly poison, rides to the Capulet tomb, and drinks the poison by Juliet's side.
* Juliet awakens, finds Romeo dead, and stabs herself with his dagger.
* Confronted with their children's deaths, the Capulets and Montagues finally end their bloody feud.

### Main Ideas and Moral Message
* The destructive nature of senseless family hatred and revenge.
* Fate vs free will (*"star-cross'd lovers"*).
* The overwhelming power and purity of youthful romantic love.

## 5. Charles Dickens – A Christmas Carol

### Author and Victorian Context
* Written by **Charles Dickens** (1812–1870), celebrated Victorian realist author and social critic.
* Published in **December 1843**; deeply influenced modern Christmas traditions and Victorian charity.

### Genre and Setting
* **Genre:** Ghost story / moral fable / social commentary novella.
* **Setting:** Victorian **London** on a freezing, foggy Christmas Eve and Christmas Day.

### Main Characters
* **Ebenezer Scrooge:** Cold, miserly, bitter old businessman (*"Bah! Humbug!"*).
* **Bob Cratchit:** Scrooge's underpaid, hardworking clerk.
* **Tiny Tim:** Bob Cratchit's kind, physically disabled young son (*"God bless us, every one!"*).
* **Jacob Marley:** Scrooge's deceased business partner, condemned in the afterlife to wander chained to heavy lockboxes.
* **The Three Spirits:** The Ghosts of Christmas Past, Christmas Present, and Christmas Yet to Come.

### Plot Summary
* On Christmas Eve, Scrooge refuses to donate to charity and mocks Christmas.
* That night, he is visited by the ghost of Jacob Marley, who warns him to change his life and predicts three spirit visits.
* **Ghost of Christmas Past:** Shows Scrooge his lonely school days and his former fiancee Belle, whom he lost due to his obsession with money.
* **Ghost of Christmas Present:** Shows the joyous, poor Christmas celebration of the Cratchit family and the allegorical children **Ignorance and Want**.
* **Ghost of Christmas Yet to Come:** A silent phantom who shows Scrooge his own lonely, unmourned death and the death of Tiny Tim.
* Scrooge wakes up on Christmas morning a changed man: he buys a giant turkey for the Cratchits, gives Bob a pay raise, donates generously to charity, and becomes a loving second father to Tiny Tim.

### Main Ideas and Moral Message
* Redemption and personal transformation.
* Compassion, charity, and social responsibility towards the poor.

## 6. Oscar Wilde – The Selfish Giant

### Author and Background
* Written by **Oscar Wilde** (1854–1900), flamboyant Irish playwright, poet, and leader of the Aesthetic Movement (*"Art for art's sake"*).
* Published in 1888 in his fairy-tale collection *The Happy Prince and Other Tales*.

### Genre and Setting
* **Genre:** Fairy tale / Christian moral allegory.
* **Setting:** A mythical village with a beautiful flowering walled garden.

### Main Characters
* **The Giant:** Selfish and harsh at first; later learns empathy and finds redemption.
* **The Village Children:** Innocent children whose laughter brings nature to life.
* **The Little Boy:** A weeping child who cannot climb a tree (later revealed as the Christ Child).

### Plot Summary
* Children love playing in the Giant's garden full of soft green grass and peach blossoms.
* The Giant returns from a seven-year visit to a friend, angrily chases the children out, and builds a high stone wall with a warning: *"TRESPASSERS WILL BE PROSECUTED"*.
* Because the children are gone, Spring refuses to visit the garden; it stays locked in eternal Winter with Frost, Snow, and North Wind.
* One morning, the Giant hears a bird singing: children have slipped in through a small hole in the wall, and the trees have burst into bloom.
* In one corner, a tiny boy is crying because he cannot reach the branches.
* The Giant's heart melts; he lifts the boy into the tree, which immediately blossoms, and breaks down the wall.
* Years pass and the Giant grows old.
* One winter morning, he sees the same little boy standing under a golden tree, bearing the wounds of nails on his hands and feet.
* The boy tells the Giant: *"You let me play once in your garden; today you shall come with me to my garden, which is Paradise."*
* The children find the Giant lying dead under the tree, covered in white blossoms.

### Main Ideas and Moral Message
* Selfishness brings loneliness and spiritual coldness.
* Love, charity, and open-heartedness bring redemption and joy.

## Miscellanea

### Key Eras of British Literature
* **Old English (5th–11th century):** Oral heroic epics (*Beowulf*).
* **Middle English (14th century):** Geoffrey Chaucer (*The Canterbury Tales*), Arthurian romance.
* **Renaissance / Elizabethan Era (16th–17th century):** William Shakespeare, Christopher Marlowe.
* **Victorian Realism (19th century):** Charles Dickens, Brontë sisters (*Jane Eyre*, *Wuthering Heights*), Thomas Hardy.
* **Aestheticism and Satire (late 19th century):** Oscar Wilde (*The Picture of Dorian Gray*).
* **20th-Century Modernism and Dystopia:** George Orwell (*1984*, *Animal Farm*), Virginia Woolf, J.R.R. Tolkien.

### Essential Literary Terms for Maturita
* **Protagonist:** The central leading character in a story.
* **Antagonist:** The character or force opposing the protagonist.
* **Climax:** The turning point and highest emotional peak of the plot.
* **Genre:** Literary category (e.g. tragedy, comedy, fable, novella, ballad, dystopia).
* **Allegory:** A story with hidden symbolic meaning beneath the surface (e.g. Dickens' Christmas spirits or Wilde's garden).

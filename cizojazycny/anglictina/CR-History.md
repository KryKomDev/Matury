# Czech History

## Legendary Past and Early Slavs

### Slavic Migration and Samo's Realm
* Slavic tribes arrived in the Czech basin in the 5th and 6th centuries during the Migration Period.
* **Samo's Realm (623–658):**
  * First tribal union of West Slavs led by Frankish merchant Samo.
  * Successfully defended against nomadic Avars and defeated Frankish King Dagobert I at the **Battle of Wogastisburg (631)**.

### Mythological Legends
* Recorded in Cosmas' medieval chronicle and Alois Jirásek's legends.
* **Forefather Čech (*Praotec Čech*):** Led his tribe to Mount Říp; declared it a land "flowing with milk and honey".
* **Princess Libuše:** Wise mythological prophetess who foresaw the fame of Prague (*"I see a great city whose glory touches the stars"*).
* Married ploughman Přemysl, founding the native **Přemyslid dynasty**.

---

## Great Moravian Empire

### First Unified Slavic State
* Existed in the 9th century across Moravia, Slovakia, and parts of neighboring lands.
* Ruled by princes **Mojmír I**, **Rostislav**, and King **Svatopluk**.
* Collapsed around 906 under attacks by nomadic Hungarian (Magyar) tribes.

### Mission of Cyril and Methodius (863 AD)
* Prince Rostislav invited scholars from the Byzantine Empire to counter Frankish political-religious influence.
* Two brothers from Thessaloniki, **Cyril (Constantine) and Methodius**, arrived in **863**:
  * Created the first Slavic alphabet: the **Glagolitic alphabet** (*hlaholice*).
  * Translated parts of the Bible and liturgy into **Old Church Slavonic**.
  * Laid the foundation for Slavic literacy and national culture.

---

## The Middle Ages: Přemyslid and Luxembourg Dynasties

### Přemyslid Dynasty and St Wenceslas
* First historical ruling dynasty of Bohemia (ruled until 1306).
* **Prince Wenceslas (*Svatý Václav*):**
  * 10th-century duke who spread Western Christianity.
  * Murdered in **935** by his brother Boleslav in Stará Boleslav.
  * Became the patron saint of the Czech nation.
* **Golden Bull of Sicily (1212):**
  * Granted to King Přemysl Otakar I by the Holy Roman Emperor.
  * Confirmed the hereditary royal title for Bohemian kings.
* Dynasty died out in 1306 when young King Wenceslas III was murdered in Olomouc.

### Charles IV and the Golden Age (1346–1378)
* House of Luxembourg ruled after John of Luxembourg married Elizabeth Přemyslid in 1310.
* **Charles IV:** Czech King and Holy Roman Emperor, known as the ***Father of the Homeland* (*Otec vlasti*)**.
* Achievements in Prague:
  * Founded **Charles University (1348)** – first university in Central Europe.
  * Founded the **New Town of Prague (1348)**.
  * Built **Charles Bridge (1357)** and began **St Vitus Cathedral (1344)**.
  * Built **Karlštejn Castle** to safe-keep crown jewels.
  * Issued the **Golden Bull of 1356**, making the Bohemian King the first secular elector of the Holy Roman Empire.

## The Hussite Revolution

### Master Jan Hus
* Roman Catholic priest, university rector, and reformer who preached in Czech at Bethlehem Chapel in Prague.
* Criticized church corruption, sale of indulgences, and excessive church wealth.
* Summoned to the **Council of Constance**; refused to abandon his beliefs.
* **Burned at the stake as a heretic on July 6, 1415**.

### The Hussite Wars (1419–1434)
* Hus's death triggered a nationwide uprising.
* Hussites used the **Chalice** (*kalich*) as their symbol (demanding communion in both kinds: bread and wine for all).
* Military commander **Jan Žižka of Trocnov**:
  * Used peasant weapons: flails and iron-pointed spears.
  * Developed the **wagon fort (*vozová hradba*)** and early firearms (*píšťala*, *houfnice*).
  * Defeated five Catholic crusades sent by the Pope and Emperor Sigismund.
* Internal split: Radicals were defeated by moderate Hussites at the **Battle of Lipany (1434)**.

### King George of Poděbrady (1458–1471)
* Only non-dynastic Czech nobleman to become King; known as the "Hussite King".
* Maintained religious peace between Catholics and Utraquists.
* Proposed an early European league of Christian kings to prevent wars.

---

## The Habsburg Dynasty (1526–1918)

### Rudolf II and Renaissance Prague
* Habsburgs assumed the Bohemian throne in 1526 under Ferdinand I.
* **Emperor Rudolf II (1576–1611):**
  * Moved the imperial seat from Vienna to **Prague**.
  * Turned Prague into a centre of arts, alchemists, and astronomers (**Tycho Brahe**, **Johannes Kepler**).

### Defenestration of 1618 and Battle of White Mountain (1620)
* **Second Defenestration of Prague (1618):** Protestant nobles threw two Catholic governors out of Prague Castle windows, sparking the **Thirty Years' War**.
* **Battle of White Mountain (*Bílá hora*, Nov 8, 1620):** Czech Protestant forces were crushed by the imperial Habsburg army.
* **Old Town Square Executions (June 21, 1621):** 27 Czech Protestant leaders were executed.

### "The Dark Age" (*Doba temna*) and Comenius
* Forced recatholicization, Germanization, and loss of Czech political independence.
* Protestant intellectuals forced into exile, including **Jan Amos Comenius** (*Teacher of Nations*).

### Maria Theresa and Joseph II (18th Century)
* Enlightened monarchs who reformed the empire:
  * Maria Theresa introduced **mandatory school attendance (1774)**.
  * Joseph II issued the **Toleration Patent (1781)** and abolished serfdom (*nevolnictví*).

---

## Czech National Revival

### Revival of Language and Culture
* Cultural and linguistic movement in the late 18th and 19th centuries to revive the dying Czech language.
* **Key leaders:**
  * **Josef Dobrovský and Josef Jungmann:** Codified modern Czech grammar and compiled dictionaries.
  * **František Palacký:** Historian known as the *Father of the Nation*.

### Building National Cultural Institutions
* Led to the construction of:
  * **National Museum (1890)** on Wenceslas Square.
  * **National Theatre (1881)**, funded by public donations with the motto *"Národ sobě"*.

---

## Czechoslovakia: The First Republic (1918–1938)

### Independence and T. G. Masaryk (October 28, 1918)
* Collapse of the Austro-Hungarian Empire after WWI.
* Creation of independent **Czechoslovakia** on **October 28, 1918**.
* Founded by **Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk** (first President, *President Liberator*), **Edvard Beneš**, and **Milan Rastislav Štefánik**.

### Democratic and Industrial Strength
* One of the few functioning parliamentary democracies in Central Europe during the 1930s.
* Strong industrial economy: Škoda, ČKD, and Baťa shoe manufacturing in Zlín.
* Challenged by internal ethnic tensions with the 3 million-strong German minority.

### The Munich Agreement (September 30, 1938)
* Germany (Hitler), Britain (Chamberlain), France (Daladier), and Italy (Mussolini) met in Munich without Czechoslovak representatives (*"About us, without us"*).
* Forced Czechoslovakia to hand over its border regions (**Sudetenland**) and fortifications to Nazi Germany.

---

## World War II and Nazi Occupation

### The Protectorate (1939–1945)
* On **March 15, 1939**, Nazi troops occupied the remaining Czech lands, establishing the **Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia**.
* Gestapo terror, closing of Czech universities on November 17, 1939, and extermination of Czech Jews (Terezín).

### Operation Anthropoid (May 27, 1942)
* Czechoslovak paratroopers trained in Britain—**Jozef Gabčík** and **Jan Kubiš**—assassinated SS General **Reinhard Heydrich** in Prague.
* Nazi reprisals: Executions, martial law, and the complete destruction of the villages of **Lidice and Ležáky**.
* Paratroopers died fighting in the crypt of Saints Cyril and Methodius Cathedral in Prague.

### Liberation and the Beneš Decrees (1945)
* Prague Uprising (May 1945); liberated by the Soviet Red Army and US Army (General Patton in Plzeň).
* Post-war **Beneš Decrees** resulted in the expulsion of roughly 3 million Germans from the border regions.

---

## The Communist Era (1948–1989)

### The Communist Coup (February 1948)
* The Communist Party led by Klement Gottwald seized absolute power (*Vítězný únor*).
* Czechoslovakia fell under strict Soviet control behind the Iron Curtain.

### 1950s Repression and Show Trials
* Nationalization of private businesses, forced farm collectivization, and censorship.
* Fabricated political show trials and executions, notably of democratic politician **Milada Horáková** (1950).

### The Prague Spring and Soviet Invasion (1968)
* Reform movement led by **Alexander Dubček**, promoting **"Socialism with a human face"** (freedom of speech, press, travel).
* **August 21, 1968:** Warsaw Pact armies invaded Czechoslovakia to crush the reforms.
* **Jan Palach (January 1969):** Student who burned himself to death on Wenceslas Square to protest against public apathy.

### Normalization and Charter 77
* Strict re-imposition of totalitarian control under Gustáv Husák.
* **Charter 77 (*Charta 77*):** Human rights manifesto published by dissidents, led by playwright **Václav Havel**.

---

## The Velvet Revolution (1989)

### Student Demonstration on November 17
* Police brutally attacked a peaceful student march on Národní třída in Prague.
* Triggered nationwide student and actor strikes and daily mass protests on Wenceslas Square and Letná Plain.
* Citizens jingled keys to signal the end of communist rule.

### Fall of the Regime and Václav Havel
* Peaceful, non-violent transition (**Velvet Revolution / *Sametová revoluce***).
* Communist government resigned; **Václav Havel** was elected President on December 29, 1989.

---

## The Czech Republic in the 21st Century

### Velvet Divorce (1993)
* Peaceful division of Czechoslovakia into two sovereign nations: the **Czech Republic** and the **Slovak Republic** on **January 1, 1993**.

### NATO and European Union Integration
* Joined **NATO** in 1999.
* Joined the **European Union** on May 1, 2004.
* Joined the Schengen Area in 2007.

## Miscellanea

### The Three Prague Defenestrations
1. **First Defenestration (1419):** Hussites threw anti-Hussite Catholic town councillors from the New Town Hall window, beginning the Hussite Wars.
2. **Second Defenestration (1618):** Protestant nobles threw two Catholic Habsburg governors from Prague Castle windows, sparking the Thirty Years' War.
3. **Third Defenestration (1948):** Foreign Minister Jan Masaryk was found dead under his bathroom window at Černín Palace shortly after the communist coup.

### Milada Horáková
* Czech lawyer, member of parliament, and WWII anti-Nazi resistance fighter.
* Imprisoned by communists and executed in a fabricated 1950 show trial despite international appeals by Albert Einstein and Winston Churchill.
* Ultimate symbol of moral courage and defense of democracy.

### The Heroism of Anthropoid Paratroopers
* Jozef Gabčík and Jan Kubiš sacrificed their lives after defending the church crypt against hundreds of SS soldiers for hours (*"We will never surrender"*).
* Their mission persuaded Britain and France to officially revoke the Munich Agreement.

### Czech Historical Identity
* Contrast between humor/skepticism (symbolized by the fictional Good Soldier Švejk) and deep historical bravery (Jan Hus, Jan Žižka, Jan Palach, Václav Havel).

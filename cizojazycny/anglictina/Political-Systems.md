# Political Systems

## Democracy: Democratic vs Non-Democratic Systems

### Definition of Democracy
* Derived from Greek words: *demos* (people) and *kratos* (power or rule) = "rule by the people".
* Abraham Lincoln's definition: *"Government of the people, by the people, for the people."*

### Core Pillars of Democracy
* **Free and fair elections:** Regular, secret ballots with universal adult suffrage.
* **Separation of powers:** Dividing power among legislative, executive, and judicial branches to prevent tyranny.
* **Rule of law (*právní stát*):** Laws apply equally to all citizens and government officials; nobody is above the law.
* **Protection of human rights:** Fundamental rights guaranteed: freedom of speech, assembly, religion, and press.
* **Political pluralism:** Multiple political parties competing openly for power.

### Direct vs Representative Democracy
* **Direct Democracy:** Citizens vote directly on laws and policies (e.g. ancient Athens, Swiss referendums).
* **Representative Democracy:** Citizens elect representatives (MPs, senators, presidents) to make decisions on their behalf.

### Authoritarian and Totalitarian Regimes
* **Authoritarianism:**
  * Power is held by a single dictator, military group, or small elite.
  * Opposition is suppressed, but some economic and private life remains outside state control.
* **Totalitarianism:**
  * The state attempts total control over all public and private aspects of life.
  * Uses terror, secret police, mass surveillance, complete censorship, and mandatory state ideology (e.g. Nazi Germany, Stalin's USSR, North Korea).

### Absolute vs Constitutional Monarchies
* **Absolute Monarchy:** The king or queen has unlimited political and legal power (e.g. Saudi Arabia).
* **Constitutional Monarchy:** The monarch's powers are strictly limited by law and parliament (e.g. UK, Spain, Sweden).

## Three Branches of Government

### Principle of Separation of Powers
* Proposed by French philosopher Montesquieu.
* Prevents concentration of power by splitting state authority into three independent branches.

### Legislative Branch (The Legislature / Parliament / Congress)
* **Main task:** Proposes, debates, amends, and passes laws; approves state budget and taxes.
* **Structure:** Usually bicameral (two chambers) or unicameral (single chamber).

### Executive Branch (The Government / Administration)
* **Main task:** Implements, enforces, and administers laws; runs foreign policy and national defense.
* **Leadership:** Led by a Prime Minister (parliamentary system) or a President (presidential system).

### Judicial Branch (The Courts / Judiciary)
* **Main task:** Interprets laws, resolves legal disputes, and protects individual constitutional rights.
* **Independence:** Judges must remain independent and protected from political pressure.
* **Judicial review:** High or Constitutional courts can strike down laws that violate the constitution.

### System of Checks and Balances
* Each branch limits the power of the other two:
  * Executive can veto legislation passed by parliament.
  * Legislature can override a veto or vote no-confidence in the government.
  * Courts can declare executive acts or parliamentary laws unconstitutional.

## The Head of State

### Monarch vs President
* **Monarch:** Hereditary position held for life; acts as an apolitical symbol of national unity and tradition.
* **President:** Elected official serving a fixed term (usually 4 or 5 years), often limited to two terms.

### Ceremonial vs Executive Presidents
* **Ceremonial / Parliamentary President (e.g. Czech Republic, Germany, Italy):**
  * Serves mainly as the formal head of state.
  * Real day-to-day power belongs to the Prime Minister and cabinet.
  * Acts on major appointments, diplomatic duties, and suspensive vetoes.
* **Executive President (e.g. USA, France):**
  * Combines the roles of Head of State and Head of Government.
  * Directly controls the cabinet, military, and federal executive agencies.

## Comparison: Czech Republic, UK, and USA

### Overview Table

| Feature | The Czech Republic | The United Kingdom | The United States |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **System** | Parliamentary Republic | Constitutional Monarchy | Federal Presidential Republic |

| **Head of State** | President (Petr Pavel) | King Charles III | President (The White House) |

| **Legislature** | **Parliament:**<br>• Chamber of Deputies (200)<br>• Senate (81) | **Parliament:**<br>• House of Commons (650)<br>• House of Lords (~800) | **Congress:**<br>• House of Representatives (435)<br>• Senate (100) |

| **Main Party Pattern**| Multi-party coalitions | Two dominant parties | Strict two-party duopoly |

### Key Differences to Highlight
* **Executive connection:** In CR and UK, the Prime Minister is dependent on parliament and can fall in a vote of no-confidence. In the USA, the President is elected separately and cannot be removed by simple disagreement.
* **Removal of leaders:** The US President can only be removed through **impeachment** (accusation by House, conviction by 2/3 of Senate).
* **Upper Chambers:** Czech Senate is elected by citizens; British House of Lords is non-elected (appointed life peers and hereditary peers); US Senate consists of 2 elected senators per state.

## Political Parties and Ideologies

### Role of Political Parties
* Connect citizens with government policy.
* Nominate candidates for elections.
* Form governments or act as official parliamentary opposition ("watchdogs").

### Left-Wing Politics (Social Democracy, Socialism, Greens)
* Emphasizes social equality and a strong welfare state.
* Supports state intervention in the economy, funded healthcare, and public education.
* Backs progressive taxation and strong worker/environmental protections.
* Examples: Labour Party (UK), Social Democrats (CR).

### Right-Wing Politics (Conservatism, Classical Liberalism)
* Emphasizes individual responsibility and personal freedom.
* Advocates for free-market capitalism, lower taxes, and deregulation.
* Promotes fiscal discipline, reduced government spending, and traditional social values.
* Examples: Conservative Party (UK), Civic Democrats / ODS (CR), Republicans / GOP (USA).

### Centrist and Green Parties
* **Centrists:** Blend market economic ideas with moderate social safety nets and progressive civil liberties (e.g. Liberal Democrats, STAN, Pirates).
* **Green Parties:** Prioritize climate action, renewable energy, and ecological sustainability.

### Coalition Governments vs Single-Party Governments
* **Coalitions:** Common in proportional systems (like CR); multiple parties must negotiate a joint programme.
* **Single-party governments:** Common in majoritarian systems (like UK and USA); one party holds a full legislative majority.

## Miscellanea: Voting Systems and Electoral Issues

### First-Past-The-Post (FPTP) / Plurality System
* Used in the **UK** and **USA**.
* **How it works:** Country is split into single-seat constituencies. The candidate with the most votes in that area wins the seat ("winner takes all").
* **Pros:**
  * Simple and fast to understand.
  * Produces stable single-party governments.
  * Direct personal link between an MP and their local constituents.
* **Cons:**
  * Millions of **"wasted votes"** for candidates who finish second or third.
  * Disproportionate results (a party can win a majority of seats with only 35% of total votes).
  * Suppresses smaller parties and reinforces a two-party monopoly.

### Proportional Representation (PR)
* Used in the **Czech Republic** (Chamber of Deputies) and most continental European countries.
* **How it works:** Parties gain seats in direct proportion to their overall national or regional vote share (usually with a 5% minimum threshold).
* **Pros:**
  * Very fair: every vote counts toward final seat counts.
  * Enables smaller and new parties to enter parliament.
  * Promotes higher voter turnout.
* **Cons:**
  * Leads almost exclusively to **coalition governments**, requiring compromises.
  * Risk of political instability or frequent cabinet crises.
  * Weaker connection between individual MPs and local geographic areas.

### Two-Round Majoritarian Run-Off System
* Used for the **Czech Senate** and **Czech / French Presidential elections**.
* A candidate must win more than 50% in Round 1.
* If nobody achieves 50%, the top two candidates go to a head-to-head second round.
* Ensures the winner has the proven majority support of participating voters.

### Current Challenges for Democracies
* **Voter apathy:** Low turnout, especially among young citizens who feel unrepresented.
* **Misinformation and fake news:** Social media algorithms and echo chambers radicalizing political debate.
* **Populism:** Political leaders promising simple answers to complex global problems while attacking democratic institutions and independent media.

# UK History

## Early History and Prehistory

### Prehistoric Britain and Stonehenge
* Earliest human hunters crossed over when Britain was connected to the European continent by a land bridge (Doggerland).
* Neolithic farmers built earthworks, burial mounds, and stone monuments.
* **Stonehenge (c. 3000–2000 BC):**
  * Megalithic stone circle located in Wiltshire.
  * Aligned with the summer and winter solstices; served as a ceremonial and astronomical site.

### The Celts and the Iron Age
* Celtic tribes arrived around 700 BC.
* Brought iron tools, built fortified hill forts, and practiced Druidic religion.
* Language and culture survive today in Welsh, Scottish Gaelic, and Irish.

---

## Invaders: Romans, Saxons, Vikings, and Normans

### Roman Britain (43–410 AD)
* Emperor Claudius launched the Roman conquest in 43 AD; Britain became the province of *Britannia*.
* **Boudica's Revolt (60/61 AD):** Celtic warrior queen who burned Roman Londinium before being defeated.
* **Hadrian's Wall (122 AD):** 117-km stone defensive wall across northern England to keep out Scottish tribes (Picts).
* **Roman legacy:** Founded **Londinium** (London), built paved roads, thermal baths (Bath), and introduced Latin and Christianity.
* Romans withdrew in 410 AD to defend Rome from barbarian attacks.

### Anglo-Saxons and Christianity
* Germanic tribes (Angles, Saxons, Jutes) arrived from Denmark and northern Germany in the 5th century.
* Pushed native Celts into Wales, Scotland, and Cornwall.
* Established the foundations of Old English and English kingdoms (Wessex, Mercia, Northumbria).
* **Christianization:** Pope Gregory sent Saint Augustine to Kent in 597 AD; became the first Archbishop of Canterbury.

### The Vikings and the Danelaw
* Norse raids began in 793 AD with the attack on Lindisfarne monastery.
* Conquered eastern and northern England, establishing their own territory called the **Danelaw** (centered around York / *Jorvik*).
* **King Alfred the Great of Wessex:** Stopped Viking expansion, preserved Anglo-Saxon culture, codified English laws, and united the English people.

### The Norman Conquest (1066)
* **Battle of Hastings (1066):** **William, Duke of Normandy ("William the Conqueror")** defeated King Harold Godwinson.
* William was crowned King of England in Westminster Abbey on Christmas Day 1066.
* **Impact of the Conquest:**
  * Norman French became the language of the court, creating Middle English.
  * Introduced continental feudalism and stone castles (including the Tower of London).
  * **Domesday Book (1086):** First complete national survey of land, property, and wealth.

## The Middle Ages

### Magna Carta (1215)
* King John was forced by rebellious barons to seal the **Magna Carta** (The Great Charter) at Runnymede.
* Established that **the King is not above the law**.
* Protected against arbitrary taxation and guaranteed the right to a fair trial.

### The Hundred Years' War (1337–1453)
* Long conflict between England and France over claims to the French throne.
* Famous English longbow victories at **Crécy (1346)** and **Agincourt (1415)** under Henry V.
* Eventually won by France, rallied by Joan of Arc; England lost all French territories except Calais.

### The Black Death (1348–1349)
* Bubonic plague wiped out one-third to one-half of the population.
* Created labour shortages, weakening the feudal system and triggering the Peasants' Revolt (1381).

### Wars of the Roses (1455–1485)
* Civil war between two rival branches of the royal family:
  * House of **Lancaster** (symbolized by the Red Rose).
  * House of **York** (symbolized by the White Rose).
* Ended at the **Battle of Bosworth Field (1485)**: Henry Tudor defeated King Richard III.

---

## The Tudors (1485–1603)

### Henry VII (1485–1509)
* First Tudor monarch; unified the feuding houses by marrying Elizabeth of York.
* Created the **Tudor Rose** emblem and restored royal finances.

### Henry VIII (1509–1547) and the Reformation
* Famous for having **six wives** (*Divorced, Beheaded, Died; Divorced, Beheaded, Survived*).
* Broke away from the Pope when denied an annulment of his marriage to Catherine of Aragon.
* Passed the **Act of Supremacy (1534)**: Established the Church of England with the King as its Supreme Head.
* Dissolved and seized the property of Catholic monasteries.

### Mary I ("Bloody Mary", 1553–1558)
* Devout Catholic queen who restored Catholicism and burned roughly 300 Protestants at the stake.

### Elizabeth I ("The Virgin Queen", 1558–1603)
* Restored Protestantism (Anglican Church).
* **Defeat of the Spanish Armada (1588):** English fleet crushed King Philip II's invasion fleet, establishing England as a naval power.
* **Golden Age of Culture:** Flourishing of Renaissance drama, poetry, and theatre (**William Shakespeare**).
* Exploration: Sir Francis Drake sailed around the world; early colonies founded in America.

---

## The Stuarts and Civil War (1603–1714)

### James I and the Gunpowder Plot (1605)
* King of Scotland who inherited the English throne, uniting the crowns in personal union.
* **Gunpowder Plot (Nov 5, 1605):** Guy Fawkes and Catholic conspirators attempted to blow up Parliament.

### English Civil War (1642–1651)
* Conflict between King Charles I (demanding absolute rule) and Parliament.
* **Royalists ("Cavaliers")** fought **Parliamentarians ("Roundheads")**.
* Parliament won under Oliver Cromwell; **King Charles I was executed (beheaded) in 1649**.

### Oliver Cromwell and the Commonwealth
* England declared a republic (The Commonwealth).
* Oliver Cromwell ruled as Lord Protector under strict military Puritan rule (banned theatre and Christmas celebrations).

### The Restoration (1660) and Glorious Revolution (1688)
* Monarchy restored under **Charles II** in 1660.
* Survived the Great Plague (1665) and Great Fire of London (1666).
* **Glorious Revolution (1688):** Catholic King James II was replaced without bloodshed by Protestant Prince William of Orange and Mary II.
* **Bill of Rights (1689):** Established a permanent constitutional monarchy and parliamentary supremacy.
* **Act of Union (1707):** Officially merged the kingdoms of England and Scotland into Great Britain.

---

## The Georgians (1714–1837)

### Rise of the Prime Minister
* German-born King George I spoke little English; power shifted to ministers.
* **Sir Robert Walpole** became the first de facto Prime Minister.

### Loss of the American Colonies
* American Revolutionary War (1775–1783): 13 American colonies rebelled against British taxes ("No taxation without representation").
* Declaration of Independence signed in 1776; British defeat recognized in 1783.

### Napoleonic Wars (1803–1815)
* Britain led coalitions against Napoleon Bonaparte.
* **Battle of Trafalgar (1805):** Admiral Lord Nelson defeated French and Spanish navies, securing British control of the seas.
* **Battle of Waterloo (1815):** The Duke of Wellington decisively defeated Napoleon in Belgium.

---

## The Industrial Revolution

### Inventions and Coal Power
* Britain was the birthplace of the Industrial Revolution in the late 18th century.
* Abundant supplies of coal and iron.
* Key inventions: James Watt's improved steam engine, Spinning Jenny, power loom.

### Transportation and Urbanization
* Extensive network of canals and railways built across the country.
* George Stephenson designed the first successful steam locomotive (*The Rocket*).
* Mass population migration from countryside villages into fast-growing industrial cities (Manchester, Birmingham, Leeds).

### Social Consequences
* Dangerous factory work, child labour, long hours, and poor sanitation.
* Led to early trade union movements and social novels by writers like Charles Dickens.

---

## The Victorian Age and Empire (1837–1901)

### Queen Victoria's Reign
* Reigned for 63 years during Britain's peak industrial, technological, and imperial dominance.
* The Great Exhibition of 1851 at the Crystal Palace in London showed British inventions to the world.

### The British Empire
* Largest empire in world history: *"The sun never sets on the British Empire."*
* Covered roughly one quarter of the global population and landmass.
* Key territories: India ("the Jewel in the Crown"), Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Kenya, South Africa.
* Queen Victoria was declared Empress of India in 1876.

---

## World War I and World War II

### World War I (1914–1918)
* Britain entered the war in August 1914 to defend Belgian neutrality against Germany.
* Deadly trench warfare on the Western Front (e.g. Battle of the Somme, 1916).
* Led to social changes: women working in factories gained the right to vote in 1918.

### World War II (1939–1945)
* Declared war on Nazi Germany on September 3, 1939, following the invasion of Poland.
* **Winston Churchill** became Prime Minister in May 1940; gave historic speeches inspiring national resistance (*"We shall fight on the beaches"*).
* **Dunkirk Evacuation (1940):** Over 330,000 Allied troops rescued across the Channel.
* **Battle of Britain (1940):** RAF fighter pilots defeated the German Luftwaffe, preventing Nazi invasion.
* **The Blitz (1940–1941):** Relentless nighttime bombing of London and industrial cities.
* **D-Day (June 6, 1944):** Allied landings in Normandy, liberating Western Europe.

---

## Post-War Era to the 21st Century

### The Welfare State and NHS
* 1945 Labour government under Clement Attlee built the welfare state.
* Founded the **National Health Service (NHS)** in 1948, providing free healthcare for all citizens.

### Decolonization and the Commonwealth
* Peaceful or conflict-driven independence for British colonies (India/Pakistan in 1947, African nations in the 1950s–60s).
* Empire transitioned into the **Commonwealth of Nations**.

### The Troubles in Northern Ireland (1960s–1998)
* Violent conflict between Protestant unionists and Catholic republicans.
* Ended with the historic **Good Friday Agreement (1998)** establishing power-sharing.

### Margaret Thatcher's Era (1979–1990)
* First female Prime Minister ("The Iron Lady").
* Promoted free-market privatization, reduced power of trade unions, and won the Falklands War (1982).

### 21st Century Milestones
* London hosted the 2012 Summer Olympic Games.
* **Brexit (2016 referendum, left 2020):** UK voted to leave the European Union.
* Death of Queen Elizabeth II in 2022; succession of King Charles III.

## Miscellanea

### The Princes in the Tower (1483)
* Mysterious disappearance of 12-year-old King Edward V and his brother in the Tower of London.
* Long blamed on their ambitious uncle, King Richard III.

### The Great Fire of London (1666)
* Started in a bakery on Pudding Lane.
* Destroyed 13,000 wooden houses and medieval Old St Paul's Cathedral.
* Helped eliminate the Great Plague by killing disease-carrying rats.
* Sir Christopher Wren rebuilt the city in stone, designing the new St Paul's Cathedral.

### British Inventions that Changed the World
* Steam engine (James Watt).
* Steam railway locomotive (George Stephenson).
* Telephone (Alexander Graham Bell).
* Television (John Logie Baird).
* Penicillin (Alexander Fleming).
* Computer science and Turing Machine (Alan Turing).
* World Wide Web (Tim Berners-Lee).

### Winston Churchill as a National Icon
* Voted the "Greatest Briton of all time" in a national BBC poll.
* Celebrated for leadership in WWII, his writings (Nobel Prize in Literature 1953), and famous oratory.

# The United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland

## Geography

### Location and Surrounding Waters
* Island nation located in north-western Europe.
* Made up of two main islands: **Great Britain** and **Ireland** (north-eastern part).
* Includes many smaller islands: Isle of Wight, Hebrides, Orkneys, Shetlands.
* Surrounded by:
  * **Atlantic Ocean** to the north and west.
  * **North Sea** to the east.
  * **Irish Sea** between Great Britain and Ireland.
  * **English Channel** (*La Manche*) to the south, separating the UK from France.
  * Connected to mainland Europe via the **Channel Tunnel** (*Chunnel*).

### Area and Landscape
* Total area: **243,600 km²** (approx. 3 times larger than the Czech Republic).
* Divided into two distinct physical zones:
  * **Highlands (North and West):** Rugged, rocky, and mountainous.
  * **Lowlands (South and East):** Rolling hills, fertile valleys, and plains.

### Mountain Ranges and Highest Peaks
* **Pennines:** Called the "backbone of England", runs down northern England.
* **Cumbrian Mountains:** Located in the scenic Lake District.
* **Cambrian Mountains:** Located in Wales.
* **Scottish Highlands:** Rugged mountains in northern Scotland.
* **Highest peaks:**
  * **Ben Nevis:** Highest peak in the entire UK (1,345 m), in Scotland.
  * **Yr Wyddfa / Mount Snowdon:** Highest peak in Wales (1,085 m).
  * **Scafell Pike:** Highest peak in England (978 m).

### Rivers and Lakes
* Rivers are relatively short due to the island geography.
* **River Severn:** Longest river in the UK (354 km), flows through Wales and western England.
* **River Thames:** Most famous river (346 km), flows through Oxford and London into the North Sea.
* Other notable rivers: Trent, Mersey, Clyde.
* Lakes are called *lochs* in Scotland and *loughs* in Northern Ireland.
* **Lough Neagh:** Largest lake by surface area in the UK (Northern Ireland).
* **Loch Lomond:** Largest lake on the island of Great Britain (Scotland).
* **Loch Ness:** Deepest and most famous lake, known for the legend of the monster "Nessie".
* **Lake Windermere:** Largest natural lake in England, located in the Lake District.

## Political Divisions and Capitals

### The Four Countries
1. **England:**
   * **Capital:** **London** (also the capital of the whole UK).
   * Largest, wealthiest, and most populous of the four countries.
2. **Scotland:**
   * **Capital:** **Edinburgh** (famous for Edinburgh Castle and fringe festivals).
   * Has its own legal system, educational system, and parliament at Holyrood.
3. **Wales (*Cymru*):**
   * **Capital:** **Cardiff** (major port city and castle site).
   * Strong Celtic heritage, bilingual status, and its own parliament (*Senedd*).
4. **Northern Ireland:**
   * **Capital:** **Belfast** (historic shipbuilding centre, site of *Titanic* construction).
   * Located on the island of Ireland; shares a land border with the Republic of Ireland.

### Key Geographic Terminology
* **Great Britain:** The geographical island containing England, Scotland, and Wales.
* **The United Kingdom:** The sovereign state comprising Great Britain and Northern Ireland.
* **The British Isles:** The archipelago including the UK, the Republic of Ireland, and smaller surrounding islands.
* **Crown Dependencies:** Self-governing territories linked to the British Crown (Isle of Man, Jersey, Guernsey).

## Climate and Weather

### Climate Characteristics
* **Temperate maritime climate** (mild, humid, and oceanic).
* Strongly moderated by the **Gulf Stream** (North Atlantic Current).
* Keeps winters warmer and summers cooler than other regions at similar latitudes.

### Seasons and Weather Patterns
* **Winters:** Mild and rainy; temperatures rarely drop far below freezing (around 2–7 °C).
* **Summers:** Cool to warm; average temperatures between 18–22 °C.
* **Rainfall:** Distributed throughout the year; western regions (Scotland, Wales, Lake District) receive far more rain than the eastern lowlands.
* **Changeability:** Weather changes quickly from sun to rain due to Atlantic fronts.
* **Cultural role:** Talking about the weather is the primary icebreaker and small-talk topic in Britain.

## Population and Languages

### Demographics
* Total population: approx. **68 million people**.
* Very densely populated, especially in England (home to roughly 84% of all UK residents).
* Heavily urbanized; majority live in large metropolitan areas.

### Languages
* **Official language:** **English**, spoken by the vast majority.
* **Regional Celtic languages:**
  * **Welsh (*Cymraeg*):** Spoken by ~25% of people in Wales; official status on road signs and in schools.
  * **Scottish Gaelic:** Spoken in parts of the Scottish Highlands and Western Isles.
  * **Scots:** Germanic sister language of English, spoken in Lowland Scotland.
  * **Irish Gaelic:** Spoken in parts of Northern Ireland.
  * **Cornish:** Revived Celtic language in Cornwall.

### Multicultural Society
* Highly diverse ethnic composition resulting from the British Empire and Commonwealth history.
* Major communities from India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, the Caribbean, and Nigeria.
* Major multi-ethnic cities: London, Birmingham, Manchester, Leicester.
* Celebrations of multiculturalism: Notting Hill Carnival, Diwali, Chinese New Year.

## Economy

### Service and Finance Sector
* Services generate roughly **80% of UK GDP**.
* **Financial and banking hub:** The **City of London** and Canary Wharf form a top global financial centre alongside New York.
* Other strong services: legal, insurance, software, higher education, tourism, and media.

### Industry and Advanced Manufacturing
* Aerospace and defence: Rolls-Royce aircraft engines, BAE Systems.
* Automotive: Luxury brands (Bentley, Rolls-Royce, Aston Martin, Jaguar Land Rover) and assembly plants (Mini, Nissan).
* Pharmaceuticals and biotechnology: AstraZeneca, GlaxoSmithKline (GSK).
* Chemicals and precision instruments.

### Energy and Resources
* Historic oil and natural gas production in the North Sea (now declining).
* Global leader in **renewable offshore wind energy**.
* Nuclear energy and green transition projects.

### Agriculture
* Highly mechanized and modern, but employs only ~1% of the workforce.
* Main products: wheat, barley, potatoes, dairy, beef, and sheep farming.

## Political System

### Constitutional Monarchy
* Parliamentary democracy combined with a constitutional monarchy.
* Has an **uncodified constitution** (composed of historical statutes, common law, precedents, and conventions).
* Core historical documents: **Magna Carta (1215)**, **Bill of Rights (1689)**.

### The Monarch (Head of State)
* Currently **King Charles III**.
* "The monarch reigns but does not rule" – serves as a ceremonial figurehead and symbol of unity.
* Key duties: opening Parliament, granting formal Royal Assent to bills, meeting weekly with the Prime Minister.

### The Prime Minister and Government (Executive)
* Head of government; leader of the majority party in the House of Commons.
* Official residence: **10 Downing Street** in London.
* Appoints the Cabinet ministers to manage state departments.

### The UK Parliament (Legislative)
* Sits at the **Palace of Westminster** in London.
* Divided into two houses:
  * **House of Commons:**
    * 650 Members of Parliament (MPs).
    * Elected directly by citizens using the **First-Past-The-Post** voting system.
    * Holds true legislative power: debates, drafts, and passes laws.
  * **House of Lords:**
    * Non-elected revising chamber (approx. 800 members).
    * Composed of Life Peers, hereditary peers, and Anglican bishops.
    * Reviews bills, suggests amendments, and can delay legislation.

### Devolution
* Transfer of power to regional parliaments:
  * Scottish Parliament at Holyrood (Edinburgh).
  * Welsh Parliament (*Senedd*) in Cardiff.
  * Northern Ireland Assembly at Stormont (Belfast).

## Sightseeing

### London Highlights
* **Palace of Westminster and Big Ben (Elizabeth Tower):** The Gothic seat of Parliament on the Thames.
* **Tower of London:** 900-year-old medieval castle, former royal prison, and home of the **Crown Jewels**, guarded by the Beefeaters.
* **Tower Bridge:** Iconic Victorian suspension and bascule bridge next to the Tower.
* **Buckingham Palace:** Official London residence of the monarch; famous for the Changing of the Guard.
* **Westminster Abbey:** Coronation church and burial place of British kings, queens, and historical figures.
* **Free Museums and Galleries:** British Museum, Natural History Museum, Science Museum, Tate Modern, National Gallery.
* **Parks:** Hyde Park (Speakers' Corner), Regent's Park, St James's Park.

### Stonehenge (Wiltshire)
* Megalithic stone monument dating back over 4,500 years (c. 3000–2000 BC).
* Aligned with the winter and summer solstices; built for ancient spiritual and astronomical rituals.

### Edinburgh Castle and Royal Mile (Scotland)
* Historic fortress built on a volcanic rock dominating the Scottish capital.
* Holds the Scottish Crown Jewels (*Honours of Scotland*) and the Stone of Destiny.
* Connected via the cobblestoned Royal Mile to the Palace of Holyroodhouse.

### The Lake District (Cumbria)
* England's premier national park, famed for glacial lakes, green fells, and walking trails.
* Deeply connected to Romantic poet William Wordsworth and children's author Beatrix Potter.

### Oxford and Cambridge ("Oxbridge")
* Oldest university towns in the English-speaking world (founded in the 12th and 13th centuries).
* Known for medieval and Gothic colleges, magnificent chapels (King's College), and traditional wooden boat punting.

## Miscellanea

### The Union Flag ("Union Jack")
* Formed by combining three historical national flags:
  * **St George's Cross:** Red cross on white background (England).
  * **St Andrew's Cross:** White diagonal cross on blue background (Scotland).
  * **St Patrick's Cross:** Red diagonal cross on white background (Ireland).
  * Wales is not included because it was already part of the Kingdom of England when the flag was designed; Wales uses its own **Red Dragon** flag (*Y Ddraig Goch*).

### National Floral Emblems
* **England:** Red Rose (Tudor Rose).
* **Scotland:** Thistle.
* **Wales:** Daffodil and Leek.
* **Northern Ireland:** Shamrock (three-leaf clover).

### Famous Personalities
* **Monarchy and Politics:** Queen Elizabeth II (reigned 70 years), Queen Victoria, Winston Churchill (wartime Prime Minister).
* **Science and Inventions:** Sir Isaac Newton (gravity), Charles Darwin (evolution), Alexander Fleming (penicillin), Alan Turing (computing and codebreaking), Tim Berners-Lee (World Wide Web).
* **Literature:** William Shakespeare, Charles Dickens, Agatha Christie, J.K. Rowling.
* **Music:** The Beatles, Queen, The Rolling Stones, Pink Floyd, Adele, Ed Sheeran.

### British Food and Customs
* **Full English Breakfast:** Fried eggs, bacon, sausages, baked beans, mushrooms, grilled tomatoes, toast.
* **Fish and Chips:** Deep-fried battered fish served with chunky potato chips and mushy peas.
* **Sunday Roast:** Roast meat served with roast potatoes, Yorkshire pudding, vegetables, and gravy.
* **Afternoon Tea:** Black tea served with scones, clotted cream, strawberry jam, and sandwiches.
* **Pub Culture:** The local pub is a central meeting place for casual social gatherings and weekly pub quizzes.
* **Holidays:** Bonfire Night / Guy Fawkes Night (November 5th fireworks), Boxing Day (December 26th).

### Sports
* Birthplace of major modern sports: football (soccer), rugby, cricket, tennis, golf.
* **Wimbledon:** Oldest tennis tournament in the world, played on grass courts with a traditional white dress code.
* **Premier League:** The most popular domestic football league in the world.
* **The Open Championship:** Oldest golf tournament, rooted in St Andrews, Scotland.
